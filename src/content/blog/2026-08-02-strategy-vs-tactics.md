One of the major things I took away from the book *The 12-Week Year: Get More Done in 12 Weeks Than Others Do in 12 Months* by Brian Moran and Michael Lennington was the concept of "Strategy" vs. "Tactics".

In the context of the authors' system, this largely pertains to one's overarching goals/vision controlling the *strategy* of how we move through life. Whereas the day-to-day activities we take to achieve these goals are the *tactics*.

As I've leaned hard into agentic AI to both learn more and boost my output, I keep thinking of this concept. In a huge way, LLMs unlock a developer's ability to focus on strategy far more than before. The **tactics** of how to debug a line of code, organize a module, or onboard to a new service have given way to the **strategy** of debugging *systems*, organizing *projects*, and onboarding to *completely new stacks*. It's an uncertain time where the nature of development is changing, but it's nice to recognize that as technology scales up, goals can get loftier with it.
